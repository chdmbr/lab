1Sieve analysis: concepts and terms

What it is, and the words you must know.

What is sieve analysis?

A stack of sieves with decreasing hole size (coarsest on top) separates a powder into size fractions. Weighing each fraction gives the particle size distribution (PSD).

What are oversize and undersize? What does −150/+106 mean?
  • Oversize: larger than the aperture, retained on the sieve.
  • Undersize: smaller, passes through.
  • −150/+106: passed the 150 µm sieve, retained on 106 µm. Mean size ≈ 128 µm.
What is liberation size and why does it matter?

The grain size at which the valuable mineral is freed from the gangue. Coarser: locked particles that can’t be separated. Finer: wasted grinding energy and slimes.

Say it like this: Grind just enough to liberate, no finer.
What are valuable mineral and gangue?

Valuable mineral = the part you want (galena PbS, sphalerite ZnS). Gangue = worthless waste rock.

What is blinding?

Near-aperture-size particles wedge in or stick to the holes (van der Waals, static) and clog them. Open area falls, readings go wrong. Cure: Ro-Tap tapping, dry sample, don’t overload, brush gently.

What is the Tyler series and what is a mesh number?
  • Apertures follow a geometric series, ratio √2 ≈ 1.414: equal spacing on a log scale.
  • Mesh = openings per linear inch. Bigger number, finer sieve.
What are d50 and d80?

d50: size below which 50% of the sample by weight lies (median). d80: size below which 80% lies, the standard grind-size indicator.

Can sieving separate very fine powder?

No. Below about 38 µm (20 µm with micromesh), blinding and agglomeration take over. Use sedimentation instead.

What is the first step in processing an ore?

Comminution (crushing and grinding) to reach liberation.

2Sedimentation and decantation: concepts and terms

Gravity, Stokes, and why dispersant.

Define sedimentation, decantation, supernatant.
  • Sedimentation: settling of solids under gravity in a liquid.
  • Decantation: gently pouring or siphoning off the liquid above the sediment.
  • Supernatant: the clear liquid above the sediment (it may still carry fines).
What is a dispersant? Why sodium oxalate?

An additive that stops fine particles from clumping. Oxalate adsorbs on quartz, gives equal negative charge, and the particles repel. Clumps would settle faster and give wrong sizes. Opposite: flocculant.

What is terminal velocity?

The constant speed reached when gravity = buoyancy + viscous drag. Bigger particles have a higher terminal velocity.

State the Stokes’ law assumptions.
  • Smooth spheres
  • No interaction between particles
  • Constant temperature (viscosity)
  • Terminal velocity reached at once
  • Purely viscous drag (laminar, Re < 0.2)
Free vs hindered settling?

Free: dilute, particles settle independently (ours, ≈ 0.04 vol%). Hindered: concentrated (> 5–10 vol%); upward liquid flow and collisions slow settling.

Why an upturned siphon, and why not disturb the bed?

It draws liquid from above its tip with no downward suction. Stirring the bed would carry coarse settled particles into the fine fraction.

What is the cut size idea?

After a set time, particles bigger than a certain size have settled below the siphon tip. The supernatant holds only the finer ones.

3Leaching: concepts and terms

Iron out of bauxite with HCl.

What is leaching? What is lixiviant, PLS, residue?

Dissolving a component out of a solid with a liquid. Lixiviant = leach liquid (HCl). PLS = pregnant leach solution (liquid with the dissolved metal). Residue = leftover solid.

What did we leach, from what, with what?

Iron (the impurity) out of bauxite using dilute HCl at 70 °C. Not copper.

What is bauxite and why remove iron?

The main ore of aluminium: gibbsite, boehmite, diaspore, with Fe oxides (hematite, goethite), clays and TiO₂. Removing iron gives purer alumina (aluminium metal comes later by smelting).

What variables affect leaching?

Temperature, agitation, particle size (smaller = more surface area), acid concentration, L/S ratio, time.

What is AAS?

Free atoms in a flame absorb light at the element’s characteristic wavelength. More atoms, more absorbance. Concentration is read from a calibration curve. Samples are diluted into range.

Why does [Fe] flatten out?

The leachable iron is used up, the acid is depleted, or equilibrium/product layer limits it. Fast at first (fresh surface, strong acid).

H₂SO₄ instead of HCl: how do safety precautions change?

H₂SO₄ heats strongly on dilution (add acid to water), is a dehydrating agent and burns badly. HCl mainly gives corrosive fumes, so use a hood.

What is the shrinking core model?

The reaction proceeds on the surface of an unreacted core that shrinks. Rate controlled by film diffusion, product-layer (ash) diffusion, or chemical reaction.

4Cementation: concepts and terms

Iron displaces copper.

What is cementation?

A redox displacement reaction: a more reactive metal (iron) displaces a less reactive one (copper) from solution. Fe dissolves; Cu²⁺ deposits as copper (“cement copper”). Cu²⁺ + Fe → Cu + Fe²⁺.

Why does iron displace copper?

Iron is lower in the series (E° = −0.44 V); copper is higher (+0.34 V). ΔE° = 0.78 V, spontaneous. Iron is oxidised, copper ions are reduced.

Why acid drops and why stirring?

Acid: conductivity and a clean iron surface (too much wastes iron as H₂). Stirring: thins the diffusion layer so Cu²⁺ reaches the iron faster (mass-transfer controlled).

Cementation vs electrowinning?

Why is iron use above 1:1 in practice?

Side reactions: Fe + 2H⁺ → Fe²⁺ + H₂ and Fe + 2Fe³⁺ → 3Fe²⁺.

Why isn’t a copper plate used?

Copper starts dissolved as Cu²⁺. Only the iron plate goes in.

5Electrowinning: concepts and terms

Pure copper by current.

What is electrowinning?

Recovering a metal from a solution of its ions by passing a DC current. Cu²⁺ is reduced and deposits as pure copper on the cathode. Electrolyte: CuSO₄ in H₂SO₄.

What happens at each electrode?
  • Cathode (reduction): Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
  • Anode (oxidation): 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ (acid regenerated)
Say it like this: Anode oxidises (O₂ bubbles); cathode reduces (Cu deposits).
Why is the anode inert?

The copper comes from the solution. A dissolving Cu anode would be electrorefining.

Why is current efficiency below 100%?

Side reactions: H₂ evolution first, the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ cycle, deposit loss. Efficiency = actual ÷ theoretical (Faraday).

What happens at low Cu concentration?

Cu²⁺ supply can’t carry the current, so H⁺ is reduced. Efficiency drops and the deposit is powdery.

Why the mark at 2.5 cm?

A fixed immersed area, so current density is known and runs and cathodes compare fairly.

What is current density?

Current ÷ electrode area (mA/cm²).

Electrowinning vs electrorefining?

Electrowinning: inert anode, metal from solution. Electrorefining: soluble impure anode dissolves, pure metal deposits on the cathode.

6Nylon 6,6: concepts and terms

Step-growth, interfacial.

What are monomer and polymer?

Monomer: small repeating building block (diaminohexane, adipoyl chloride). Polymer: a long chain of many monomer units (nylon 6,6).

What is step-growth polymerization?

Bifunctional monomers react in steps; any two molecules can couple (monomer, dimer, oligomer). A small molecule (HCl) is released (condensation). High molecular weight only at very high conversion. Chain-growth adds one unit to one active end.

Why “interfacial”?

Diamine + NaOH in water, adipoyl chloride in hexane: immiscible, so the reaction happens only at the boundary. Pulling the film exposes fresh interface.

Why NaOH?

It neutralises HCl. Otherwise the amine becomes –NH₃⁺ and stops reacting.

What does “6,6” mean? Can you make Nylon 6 this way?

6 carbons in the diamine + 6 in the diacid chloride. Nylon 6 = caprolactam, ring-opening: not made this way.

Why adipoyl chloride, not adipic acid?

Acid chloride reacts in seconds at room temperature. Adipic acid needs about 200 °C.

What is a limiting reagent? Which here?

The reactant that runs out first and sets the maximum product. Here the diamine (8.605 vs 15.30 mmol).

Other polymers made by step-growth?

PET (polyester), polyurethane, polycarbonate, Kevlar.
